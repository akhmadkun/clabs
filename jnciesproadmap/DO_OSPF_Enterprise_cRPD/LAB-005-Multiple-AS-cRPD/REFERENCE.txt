LAB-005 REFERENCES AND ADAPTATIONS

PRIMARY BOOK SOURCE
  Timothy Beaumont, Day One: Advanced OSPF in the Enterprise, Chapter 6,
  "Multiple Autonomous Systems", printed pages 74-84 in the uploaded PDF.

SOURCE-DERIVED DESIGN
  - The chapter adds R8 and R9 as a separate autonomous system.
  - R8 has an OSPF Area 0 loopback and an Area 1 connection to R9.
  - eBGP joins the existing network (R2) to R8 using different AS numbers.
  - First verify eBGP before exchanging any routes.
  - Export OSPF routes into BGP at R2, then inspect the routes at R8.
  - BGP-learned routes need an OSPF export policy before they appear as external
    routes in the remote OSPF database.
  - Finally reverse route exchange so AS 65002 OSPF routes reach AS 65001.
  - The book's summary cautions that multiple autonomous systems require careful
    route redistribution and tag/metric policy to avoid loops.

ADAPTATIONS FOR THIS CONTAINERLAB SERIES
  - The package continues LAB-004's six-router topology (R1-R4, R6-R7), including
    the two OSPF domains and RIB-group sharing at R1, then adds R8/R9.
  - The old book interface addresses are replaced by the lab's logical VLAN
    scheme on the shared external bridge br-univ.
  - The established convention is applied consistently: VLAN number follows the
    logical endpoint pair where practical; p2p addresses use /30 and .1/.2.
  - R1-R6 uses VLAN 16; R2-R8 eBGP uses VLAN 28; R8-R9 OSPF uses VLAN 89.
  - R9's two LAN networks use Linux endpoints EP91/EP92 instead of extra external
    networks/RIP attachments in the book. They provide real bidirectional ping
    tests after redistribution.
  - R4's static test prefixes from earlier chapters are retained. A decoy prefix
    verifies the exact-prefix export policy. R5 is omitted in this adaptation.
  - All router startup-configs contain addressing only. Protocol configuration
    is deliberately left to the learner.
