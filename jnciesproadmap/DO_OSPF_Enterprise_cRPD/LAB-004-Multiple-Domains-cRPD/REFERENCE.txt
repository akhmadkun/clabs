LAB-004 - REFERENCES AND SOURCE ADAPTATIONS

PRIMARY SOURCE
  Timothy Beaumont, Day One: Advanced OSPF in the Enterprise, Juniper Networks.
  Chapter 5, "Multiple Homogeneous Domains", printed pages 57-72 in the uploaded
  PDF. Key topics: routing instance, independent OSPF backbone, separate RIB,
  RIB groups, OSPF export policies, external route tags, and cross-domain routes.

SOURCE-DERIVED CONFIGURATION CONCEPTS
  - The book creates a virtual routing instance named domain2 on R1.
  - The added domain has an OSPF Area 0 and an Area 1, with R6 as its ABR and R7
    as its Area 1 internal router.
  - The book creates two routing-options RIB groups to share inet.0 and
    domain2.inet.0 in opposite directions.
  - The main/default OSPF process uses `rib-group sharing`; the domain2 OSPF
    process uses `rib-group sharing2`.
  - The book's policy `from_domain1` matches protocol ospf, sets external tag 11,
    and accepts; `from_domain2` matches instance domain2, sets tag 10, and accepts.
  - Route sharing and OSPF external-LSA origination are separate verification
    stages. In the book, the route tables are inspected before/after both.

LAB ADAPTATIONS (CLEARLY DISTINGUISHED FROM THE BOOK)
  - R1-R4 retain the VLAN/IP plan used by the previous cRPD labs.
  - The added Domain 2 logical links use VLAN 56 (R1 domain2 <-> R6 Area 0) and
    VLAN 67 (R6 Area 1 <-> R7). Each new cRPD eth1 parent connects to br-univ.
  - The R1 domain2 instance receives router ID 10.2.1.1 to distinguish it from
    R1's default OSPF instance in outputs.
  - R7's 172.19.21.1/32 and 172.19.22.1/32 are addressed loopbacks, advertised
    passively in Area 1. These model the book's two remote host routes.
  - All devices are cRPD using xzjt/crpd:25.2R1-S1.4; the old book used hardware
    Juniper interfaces/routers and the output commands may differ by release.
